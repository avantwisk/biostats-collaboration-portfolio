Week 2, Day 2

LISA Attitude, Relationships, and Co-authorship

Week 2
Day 2
Block 1
Communication
Relationship
Published

May 20, 2025

Session focus: LISA collaborative attitude, co-authorship norms, role-play practice, and meeting structure

Learning Objectives

  • Practice the LISA Collaborative Attitude in real scenarios
  • Strengthen relationship-building behaviors and meeting structure
  • Understand co-authorship principles and ethical collaboration norms
  • Apply analogies and plain-language explanations in role-plays

Description of Activities

On 20 May (Block 1), we focused on the LISA collaborative attitude, structured meetings (POWER), co-authorship practices, and applied communication via role-plays. Materials included checklists, analogies, and prompts to turn technical ideas into shared understanding.

Reflection (Gibbs Reflective Cycle)

1. Description

We operationalized the LISA attitude—service‑oriented, respectful, empowering—through structured meetings and role‑plays. We reviewed co‑authorship norms and used analogies to turn complex ideas into shared language.

2. Feelings

Clear behavioral cues (how to show respect, curiosity, care) grounded me. Discussing co‑authorship reduced ambiguity I’ve felt around credit and accountability. Role‑play was uncomfortable at first, then freeing as muscle memory formed.

3. Evaluation

Attitude, structure, and practice worked together. The LISA lens made small behaviors matter (greetings, agenda co‑creation, follow‑through). Co‑authorship guidance was actionable—memos at project start prevent later conflict. Analogies improved clarity without losing rigor.

4. Analysis

Attitude and relationship aren’t “soft extras”—they create the context that makes content persuasive and communication effective. I’m replacing “we can write authorship later” with “address roles early as part of structure.”

5. Conclusion

Relationship‑first doesn’t mean method‑second; it means method‑with‑purpose. Clear roles and collaborative behaviors reduce friction and raise trust, enabling better science.

6. Action Plan

I will use a standard opening (greeting, purpose, agenda, time), a brief authorship/roles memo at project start, and an analogies bank for recurring explanations. I’ll request feedback on how my behaviors support a collaborative atmosphere.

Next Reflection

Continue to Day 3 to practice questioning and consolidate skills.
